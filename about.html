Our story

Built for the people
slipping away quietly.

There is an older man who lives near me. He keeps to himself — rarely comes outside, rarely speaks to anyone. I have no idea if he has family. I have no idea if anyone calls. Some days I wonder if anyone would notice if something happened to him. That thought has never fully left me.

Around the same time, I came across a post on Reddit. Someone had tried calling their parent for days — no answer. They kept telling themselves it was fine, that their parent was probably just busy, that there was a simple explanation. There wasn't. By the time someone checked, it was too late. The post was short. The weight of it wasn't.

Then I read about a seventeen-year-old who took their own life. The details aren't mine to share. But what stayed with me was this: the signs were there. The withdrawal, the quietness, the small changes that can look like nothing until they look like everything. The people who loved that teenager didn't know what was building beneath the surface — not because they didn't care, but because no one was catching it in time.

"The signs were there. They usually are. The problem isn't caring — it's catching."

I also couldn't call my own mother every day. Work gets in the way. Life gets in the way. I'd go days thinking I should call, then feeling guilty that I hadn't, then calling on Sunday and spending the whole conversation trying to figure out what I'd missed. She'd say she was fine. I'd want to believe her.

Nori started from all of those moments at once. Not from one clean origin story, but from a slow accumulation of the same uncomfortable truth: the people we love are often not okay, and we often don't find out until it's much harder to help.

What I wanted to build was simple. Something that calls every day — not to confirm that someone is alive, but to actually talk to them. Something that notices when the mood has been shifting for two weeks, when the same worry keeps coming up, when someone mentions they haven't been sleeping but says it like it's nothing. Something that catches what a Sunday call never would.

And then tells you — not in a clinical report, but in plain language — what it heard and what you might want to pay attention to.

That's Nori. A daily call that remembers. An ear that doesn't get too busy. A signal when the quiet starts to mean something.

Sreekanth
Founder, Nori
What Nori watches for
🌫️
The quiet withdrawal

When someone stops mentioning the things they used to love. When conversations get shorter. When "I'm fine" becomes the whole answer. Nori notices the pattern across days, not just the words in one call.

🧠
The small forgetting

Asking the same question twice. Losing track of the day. Mentioning an appointment they already mentioned. These can be nothing — or they can be early signs of something that deserves attention. Nori keeps track so you don't have to.

💙
The emotional weight being carried alone

Loneliness. Grief. Hopelessness expressed quietly, wrapped in reassurance. Nori listens for what's underneath — and flags it when the conversation suggests someone is struggling more than they're letting on.

🩹
The pain being minimised

The knee that keeps coming up. The headaches mentioned in passing. The doctor's appointment that keeps getting put off. Nori remembers what was said last Tuesday — and gently follows up.

Nori is not a medical service. It is not a replacement for professional care or emergency services. It is not a substitute for the calls you make yourself.

But it is there every morning. It remembers every conversation. And when something feels different — it tells you, in time to do something about it.

That is what I wanted to build. For my neighbour. For the families on Reddit. For the seventeen-year-old's parents, who deserved to know sooner. For my mother, on all the days I couldn't call.

Give your parent someone to talk to every morning.
